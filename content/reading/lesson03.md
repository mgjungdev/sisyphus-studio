Lesson 03 · Air Through a Gap: F, V, and TH
<callout icon="🎯" color="green_bg">
	**Today's goal:** Make four sounds that Korean doesn't have: /f v θ ð/. **The air must keep flowing.** Blocking the air turns them into ㅍ, ㅂ, ㅆ, ㄷ.
	By the end, *coffee* no longer sounds like *copy*, *very* not like *berry*, and *think* not like *sink*.
</callout>
<callout icon="🗝️" color="gray_bg">
	**Marks:** ● strong beat · **·** weak beat · CAPITALS = stressed · **\|** = small pause · **‿** = link. Full key in Lesson 01.
</callout>
## 01 / Understand the pattern {color="green"}
### 1. Stops vs. flowing sounds
In Lesson 02, the air **stopped** completely (/p b t d k ɡ/). Today's sounds are **fricatives**: the air never stops. It squeezes through a narrow gap and makes friction noise. You should be able to hold each one for as long as you have breath: *ffffff, vvvvvv, θθθθθθ*.
<callout icon="🇰🇷" color="yellow_bg">
	**Why this is hard:** Korean has no sounds made with the teeth on the lip or the tongue at the teeth. So Korean replaces them with the nearest stop:
	f → ㅍ · v → ㅂ · θ → ㅆ (or ㄸ) · ð → ㄷ
	Each replacement **stops the air**. To an English listener, that is usually a different word.
	**Test:** Can you hold the sound for three seconds? If yes, it's a fricative. You can't hold ㅍ or ㅂ.
</callout>
### 2. /f/ and /v/: top teeth on the lower lip
Rest your **upper teeth lightly on your lower lip**. Blow air through the gap: that's /f/. Add your voice (throat buzz): that's /v/.
Do **not** close both lips. Closing both lips makes /p/ or /b/.

### 3. /θ/ and /ð/: tongue at the teeth
Put your **tongue tip lightly against the back of your upper teeth** (or just between the teeth). Blow air over it: that's /θ/ as in *think*. Add voice: that's /ð/ as in *this*.
You don't need to bite hard or stick your tongue far out. A gentle touch is enough, but the air **must** keep moving.

### 5. The most common TH words are small and weak
The most frequent /ð/ words are function words: *the, this, that, they, them, their, there, than, then*. In sentences they are usually **weak (·)**: short and quiet. Keep them quick, but keep the tongue at the teeth. A light /ð/ is fine; a ㄷ is not.
*the* = /ðə/ before a consonant sound (*the book*), /ði/ before a vowel sound (*the apple*).

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>
	1. /v/: top teeth on the lower lip, air flows. /b/: both lips close, air stops.
	2. No. *Thursday* has /θ/ (no voice). *Their* has /ð/ (voice).
	3. *Sink*.
	4. You can hold it: *ffff, vvvv, θθθθ*. You can't hold a stop like ㅍ or ㅂ.
	5. *them* and *their*. They are quick and light, but still /ð/.
	6. One each. 쓰리 has two; 프렌드 has three.
